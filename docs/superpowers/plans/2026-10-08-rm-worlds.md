# The Rick and Morty worlds, made immense — implementation plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Eight planets of the universe map's Rick and Morty sector become whole worlds on the galaxy's planet engine, each with its land, places, people, a ride, quests and a mission.

**Architecture:** The galaxy's surface engine (`src/components/galaxy/surface/`) gets four small seams: a site and a mission handed in by the page, a model from any url, a figure maker consulted first, and `siteFrom` factored out of `siteOf`. Everything Rick and Morty lives in `src/components/rickmorty/planets/`: a kit (catalogue, cast, props, rides, lines, HUD skin) and one site file per planet. `pages/RmPlanet.jsx` opens the new `RmSurface` for the eight and `RmWorld` for the two it keeps.

**Tech Stack:** three.js r186, React 19, Vitest, Playwright (headless Chromium, software WebGL) for checks.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-08-rm-worlds-design.md`

## Global Constraints

- British spelling, curly quotes in copy, comments that say why; files under 800 lines (`big-files`); no TODO notes; no model names in code, docs or commits.
- Rules pure (no three.js, no DOM), seeded where random, tested beside each file. Site files are data and import nothing from three.js.
- Every galaxy world plays as it did: `sites.test.js`, `validity.test.js`, `scene`'s tests and `scripts/travel-check.mjs` stay green and are not loosened.
- No new model files: every kind is a model in `public/` already, a dye of one, or built in `props.js`. No runtime call to any asset service.
- Save keys: `tp-rm-found`, `tp-rm-quests`, `tp-rm-missions`. `tp-rm-done`, `tp-galaxy-*` untouched.
- Land: `terrain.js`'s `HALF` 640 and `REACH` 590. Life under 40 figures a site; scattered instances under 4,000 a site; zones under 300 parts.
- Each phase: `npx eslint .`, `npx vitest run`, `npx vite build`, `node scripts/health.mjs --check --skip build`, and for a route touched `node scripts/autopilot-check.mjs --only smoke --skip lint,test,build --routes /c-137/<id>`; a screenshot in the PR. PR to `main`, CI green, merge commit; never force-push.
- One phase per PR, merged before the next phase starts (Phases 2–9 may run in parallel on their own branches from `main` after Phase 1).

## Review Focus

1. A planet opened with no saves at all (first visit, private window) must land, show its places on the compass and offer its first quest: Task 0.5's test mounts `RmSurface` with `local` empty.
2. A mission started, then left by taking off mid-way, must not keep the night sky or the shelling on the next landing: Task 0.3's test builds the site twice, mission then none, and the second has the site's own sky.
3. A life kind whose Meshy model fails to load must still stand (the capsule stand-in) and never throw in a frame: Task 1.2's test gives `rmFigure` a cast whose `make` returns null and expects null, and the actors' own fallback takes over (its existing test).
4. A quest's `collect` spots and a race's gates must all be on walkable ground above water and inside `REACH`: Task 2.1's site test (the template every planet copies) checks each spot with `standable` and `Math.hypot < REACH`.
5. A ride left in the oil canal or the crater's lake must come back: Task 1.4's test drives `walker.js`'s `ride` over water deeper than `tooDeep` for 4 s and expects each hover ride to stay on the surface and come out the far side (`ride` has no drowning: a hover holds over the water, so it is never lost; a flyer over deep water is in the hand-off's Left).
